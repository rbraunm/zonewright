# How the client renders a zone

What `eqgame.exe` and `EQGraphicsDX9.dll` of the RoF2 client do to draw a zone, read from their code and the client's compiled effects (`RenderEffects\*.fxo`). The zone renderer reproduces this; calibration against screenshots checks it. Addresses are the RoF2 client's. Calibration screenshots come from the live client, whose renderer may have moved on since; differences that remain after matching these rules may be that.

## Effects

The DLL draws with Direct3D 9 effects from `RenderEffects\SPL` (single pass) and `RenderEffects\MPL` (multipass). Each effect has techniques for DX9, DX8, and DX6 hardware; a technique without a pixel shader leaves the pixel stage to the DLL's texture stage states, which set color to MODULATE (texture times vertex color) and never to MODULATE2X.

A classic (WLD) zone mesh draws with `SPL\RegionOldA.fxo` (technique `RegionOldA_DX6_VS1_PS0`): a vertex shader and no pixel shader.

## Classic zone lighting

`RegionOldA`'s vertex shader lights each vertex from its stored color (RGB baked light, alpha the share of scene light it receives) and normal:

    light = vertex.rgb + vertex.a * (ambient + bounce * max(N . D, 0) + sun * max(N . -D, 0))
          + specialAmbient + sum over three point lights of color * max(N . L, 0) * (1 - min((distance * k)^2, 1))

- N is the stored normal (bytes 0-255 mapped to -1..1), D the sun direction, L the direction to a point light; k is the reciprocal of the light's radius (see [Point lights](#point-lights)).
- The pixel is the texture times the light, clamped to 1 by the fixed-function stage, then fogged.
- Fog is exponential squared per vertex: `fog = exp(-(density * clamp(10 * (distance - fogStart) / (fogEnd - fogStart), 0, 10))^2)`, distance from the eye, and the pixel is `lerp(fogColor, pixel, fog)`. The 10 is the effect's own `fFogRange`; the DLL sets start, end, and density (default 0.33), so at the default density fog reaches 63% a third of the way from start to end and is total at the end.
- **Which fog and how far** (`eqgame.exe` `0x48ae80`, `0x48ac00`): only the zone row's first fog set is read. Fog is drawn only when the zone type (`ztype`) is not 0; Highpass Hold's is 0, so it draws none. The far clip runs from the zone's minimum clip to its maximum with the player's clip slider (minclip + (maxclip - minclip) x slider x 0.05; the maximum at 20). A fog end at or past the far clip is pulled in to clip - 0.15 x (clip - start). After NewZone the client raises a minimum clip under 50 to 50. The preview draws as a player with the slider at its maximum.

So a WLD zone mesh's vertex colors are not a multiplier: in Plane of Knowledge they are near black with alpha 255 (lit by scene light), in Eastern Wastes black with alpha about 200.

## Where the lighting values come from

The DLL sets the effect parameters per draw (`0x10088cb5`) from its world object's environment (`0x1008b470`):

| Parameter | Source |
|---|---|
| `Ambient` | scene graph ambient, set by vtable `0xc8` from a D3DCOLOR (bytes / 255) |
| `SpecialAmbient` | the sum of two scene graph colors (vtable `0xcc` and `0xd0`), each channel clamped to 1 |
| `BounceColor` | scene graph bounce color (vtable `0xd4`) |
| `DirectionalColor`, `DirectionalNormal` | the world's sun object (scene graph `+0x230`, set by vtable `0xac`): color at `+0x5c`, direction at `+0x50` (`0x1008b5ef`) |
| `FogStart`, `FogEnd`, `FogDensity` | the device's fog settings (`+0xa9b8`, `+0xa9bc`, `+0xa9c0`) |

`eqgame.exe` sets the scene ambient each frame (`0x4942b0`):

- **Full bright** (a debug and vision mode): white.
- **Outdoor zones** (zone type 1, 2, or 5): the sky's ambient color when the zone has a sky object; otherwise hourly tables interpolated by the minute (`0xaf3260`, `0xaf32c0`, `0xaf3320`), red and green 0 at night rising to 0.9 at noon, blue 0.3 at night rising to 0.9.
- **Zone type 4**: 0.5 gray. **Other zone types**: 0.1 gray.
- **The viewer's floor** (`0x494461`-`0x49471d`): each channel is then raised to the floor of the character's vision: 0.08 without infravision or ultravision (`0x9c82e0`), (65, 50, 50) / 255 with infravision and (80, 80, 120) / 255 with ultravision (`0xddf808`-`0xddf81c`, set at `0x5141e0`; which the character has comes from `[player + 0x3248]`, `[player + 0x3264]`, and `0x438990` with `0x41` and `0x42`). So the sky's black night ambient reaches the scene as 0.08.
- **Special ambient** (vtable `0xcc`): the same floor times 1 - f, where f moves 0.01 a frame toward a value the player's actor gives (`[0xdd2644] + 0x101c`, vtable `0xf0`, not traced), or toward 1 without one.

The zone header (the server's NewZone packet) carries zone type, sky type, fog colors and distances (four sets), clip distances, and whether fog and sky are on. The live dumps record it in `fields\zoneHeaders.tsv`.

## Zone files as the renderer reads them

- **Vertex colors** (WLD 0x36) are stored blue first (D3DCOLOR): Plane of Knowledge's torch-lit walls bake orange only read that way.
- **Normals** are signed bytes over 127 in the preview. The client's mesh builders (`0x1001f630` regions, `0x10057060` objects, `0x1004ad50` skins) instead look each byte up in a 32-entry table (`0x100c1c10`, built at `0x100c1bb0`): `(byte >> 3) & 31` gives `k / 15` for 0-15 and `(k - 31) / 15` for 16-31, so a component steps by 1/15 and the bytes -8 to -1 read 0. The preview does not quantize yet.
- **Placed objects** (`objects.wld` 0x15): actor, position, a heading and a tilt in 512ths of a turn (the third rotation is always 0 in the zones read so far), and one scale. The renderer turns an object by its tilt about its Y axis, turning +Z toward -X, then by its heading about Z as a spawn's heading turns it: the matrix the RoF2 client's actor holds (`CSimpleActor + 0xe4` in MQPeridotEmu's dumps) for tilted placements, such as Crystal Caverns' `CCROCK204` at (-225.8, 839.0), heading 384 and tilt 326. The vertex light they draw with is in [Classic zones](#classic-zones).
- Zone and object textures are often DDS data under `.bmp` names.
- **Texture orientation:** WLD meshes and static (boneless) EQG models count v down from a texture's top, so the renderer flips them for Blender. The Neighborhood's map board and guild gate show upright only that way. Skinned EQG models (a Drakkin's face) show upright unflipped. Which DLL step makes the difference is not traced: the static model effects (`SModel*`) read texture coordinates as shorts over 256, the skinned ones (`SkinMesh*`) as floats.

## Classic zones

Traced in `eqgame.exe` and `EQGraphicsDX9.dll`, checked across every classic zone the client ships (scripts and pictures of the fidelity census) and against MQPeridotEmu's RoF2 dump of the Plane of Knowledge (`peridotEmuZone_poknowledge_0_20261004-173023.txt`, which records each actor's color count, first color, and FNV-1a hash of its colors).

### Which files load

- **Archives** (`EQGraphicsDX9.dll` `0x100dd090`): the client files an archive's entries by their key alone and finds a file by its name's key: a CRC-32 with polynomial `0x04C11DB7`, unreflected, from 0, over the name and its terminating zero (`0x100dae40`, table `0x101750b0`). The filename list only names them. `greatdivide_chr.s3d` lists 726 names for 725 entries and `velketor_chr.s3d` 916 for 915: the extra names (`growthplane_chr.wld`, `kael_chr.wld`, left from the archives they were copied from) have no entry and cannot be read, and every other file reads by its key. Across all 2126 client archives pairing names with entries by key gives what pairing them in data order gives wherever the counts agree.
- **EQG over classic** (`eqgame.exe` `0x49b200`): the client tries `<zone>.EQG` first ("Attempting to load %s.", `0x49b3e9`) and, once it loads, opens no classic zone file: the flag it sets (`0x49b4cb`) skips `<zone>_obj2`, `_obj`, `_2_obj`, `_chr2`, `2_chr`, `_chr` (`0x49b9a2`) and `<zone>.s3d` with its `objects.wld` and `lights.wld` (`0x49c25c`). The client's `Logs\dbg.txt` shows both paths: "Attempting to load highpasshold.EQG." then "Loaded highpasshold.EQG." and no classic file; "Attempting to load poknowledge.EQG." then "Loading poknowledge_obj2" and the rest. An `.eqg` loads only when one of its model, terrain, or zone files does (`EQGraphicsDX9.dll` `0x10066230` counts `0x10065c00`'s successes, which come only from files starting `EQGM`, `EQGS`, `EQAL`, `EQGL`, `EQGT`, `EQGZ`, `EQLOD`, `EQTZP`, or `EQOBG`). So the Arena and the tutorial zones (`arena`, `tutoriala`, `tutorialb`, each with a classic `.s3d` too) load as EQG zones, while `nektulos.eqg`, a byte-for-byte copy of `nektulos.s3d`, loads nothing and leaves Nektulos classic. The archives a zone links follow the variant it loads: the zone's `.eqg` first, then `<zone>_pre_chr.txt`'s, `<zone>_chr.txt`'s, and `<zone>_assets.txt`'s; on the classic path `<zone>_pre_chr.txt`'s, `poknowledge_obj3.eqg` for the Plane of Knowledge (`0x49b9dd`), `_obj2`, `_obj`, `_2_obj`, `_chr2`, `2_chr` (ten zones), `_chr`, `<zone>_chr.txt`'s, `<zone>_assets.txt`'s, and last `<zone>.s3d` with its `objects.wld` and `lights.wld` (`0x49c2d3`, `0x49c388`).
- **A Luclin zone's loose `<zone>.dat`** (`dawnshroud`, `grimling`, `hollowshade`, `letalis`, `maiden`, `mseru`, `scarlet`, `shadeweaver`, `tenebrous`, `twilight`, `umbral`): never opened. `eqgame.exe` names one `.dat` (`0x9d05e8`), the fallback for `<zone>_switches.txt` (`0x512850`, from `0x519100`); `EQGraphicsDX9.dll`'s `.dat` names are the sky's `stars.dat` and the EQ terrain system's files inside a zone's `.eqg` (`0x1010c0d0`, `0x1010c480`, `0x1010cd90`, `0x1010d040`, `Water.dat`, `InvW.dat`, `FloraExclusion.dat`), and neither binary builds `.dat` as an immediate. `eqgame.exe` loads only `EQGraphicsDX9.DLL`, not the older `EQGfx_Dx8.dll` beside it. The files hold 32-byte records (two points 2-10 units apart, a size, and a 0), mostly in threes crossing at one center, as crossed flora cards would; the RoF2 client draws none of them. `importZone` names them in `filesTheClientNeverOpens`.

### Meshes

The client parses a mesh (0x36, `0x100bfae0`) with a count for each array and builds its vertices by vertex index alone, whatever the array counts:

- **Arrays longer than the vertices:** the first one per vertex is read. Thirteen drawn region meshes store more normals and colors than vertices (Acrylia's `R17` 128 for 120; Ssraeshza Temple, Grimling Forest, the Halls of Honor, Tenebrous Mountains).
- **No UVs:** every vertex at (0, 0), so the whole mesh takes the texture's corner (regions `0x1001f630`, objects `0x10057060`, skins `0x1004ad50`). Six drawn meshes store none: the Plane of Innovation's clock hands (`BOX01`, `BOX02` skins of `POICLOCK500`), the Plane of Air's wine rack (`CYLINDER01` of `POAWINE500`), the Plane of Time's `TMTMNTNCOLID500`, and two character meshes.
- **No normals:** zero normals (no drawn mesh in the client stores none).
- **No colors:** a region vertex takes `0xFF1F1F1F` (`0x1001f7d2`, with a second color `0xFF808080` the region effect does not read), an object vertex `0xFFFFFFFF` (`0x10057060`). A skin has no color at all: the client builds skins as D3DX meshes of position, normal, and one texture coordinate (vertex format `0x112`, `0x1004ae5f`), and `SkinMeshOld` lights them by scene light alone, as a vertex with no baked light and the full share would be.
- No mesh in the client stores fewer UVs, normals, or colors than vertices (the client would read past them); the import refuses one.

### Placements and their vertex light

- **Layout** (`0x1001cad0`): flags `0x1` shift the fields by one more, `0x2` stores the position and three turns and one more float, `0x4` and `0x8` two scales, `0x80` a flag the actor keeps, and `0x100` a reference to the placement's own vertex colors: a color track (0x33) naming its colors (0x32: a flag, a count, three more fields, then a D3DCOLOR per vertex; `0x100bfdf0`). 81,923 placements in 114 classic zones carry them, and every one of their color definitions has the layout 1, n, 1, 200, 0.
- **A static actor** (`CSimpleActor`, `0x10053820`) hands the colors to its model (`0x10054880`), which copies them whole. When it builds its draw buffers (`0x10054630`, `0x1008f6d0`) a model holding colors puts colors[i] on its mesh's vertex i, in place of the mesh's own, with no check of the count. Seven placements in three zones carry fewer colors than their mesh has vertices (Dalnir's `KSSTATUE103` 353 for 367, North Freeport's `BARRELONSIDE` 78 for 81, Neriak Commons' `STONEMAN` 22 for 389): the client reads on past the copy into whatever its memory pool holds next (`0x10012fd0`, a pool that hands out consecutive blocks), which cannot be known from the files. The preview gives those vertices the mesh's own light and the import names the actors in `placementColorsShort`. 66 placements carry more colors than vertices; the rest are unread.
- **A skeletal actor** (`CHierarchicalActor`, `0x10044550`) never reads its placement's colors (three placements carry them) and has no model the light choice asks after (`0x10102930` returns none), so it takes every light.
- **Load-time light:** a static actor placed without colors of its own gets colors the client computes once the zone has loaded ("Performing post-load operations", `eqgame.exe` `0x49b200`, engine `+0x24`, `EQGraphicsDX9.dll` `0x10085a50`, `0x10068b70`, then the actor's `0x100530f0`), which replace the mesh's own (`0x100548d0`). `loadTimeLight` computes them and the import draws them, counting the placements in `placementsLitAtLoad` (140,155 in the client's classic zones, each placing an actor of one mesh with normals):
  - **Alpha** is the actor's share of scene light times 255, truncated (`0x1005313f` sets the FPU to truncate). The share comes from a drop (`0x1003a4c0`) from the model's bounding sphere: the box the mesh fragment (0x36) stores about its center, its center and half its diagonal (the radius the dumps show each actor holding at `+0x144`), set at the actor's position, neither turned nor scaled (`0x100532c0`). Eight sweeps are tried in turn (`0x1003a580`-`0x1003a6d9`; constants `0x10131fcc`, `0x10136810`-`0x10136818`, `0x1013680c`, `0x10133610`, `0x10133618`): from the sphere's top to 20 below its bottom; from 20 above its top to its top; from its bottom to 400 below; from 100 above its top to its top; then from 100 above its top to 400 below its bottom, 10 units off along +x, -x, +y, and -y. The first that lands gives the floor, whose share is the sum of its corners' alpha / 256 (the table at `[0x1017910c] + 0x85a0`) times the DLL's third, `0x101338cc` (`0x1001fa60`), and at least 0.1 (`0x1013377c`); with none landing the share is 0.1. Measured against the dumps, not traced: a sweep is a sphere of radius 0.5 (the query's `0x10131c1c`) that lands only on polygons facing up that players do not pass through and whose material is not invisible (render method 0), and the share is rounded toward zero to single precision. In Grimling Forest's cave the sweeps under `LROCK311A` at (929.8, 1863.9) meet only an invisible polygon, so it takes 0.1 (alpha `0x19`); the rocks on the cave floor take its 0.27 to 0.36.
  - **RGB** (`0x10010260`): the zone lights whose sphere reaches the actor's, the light's radius plus the bounding radius times the placement's scale (`0x100c3f00`) from the actor's position, light each vertex within a light's radius that faces it by the light's color times 0.05 radius^2 / distance^2 times the facing (`0x10132e6c`); the sum is accumulated in single precision, times 255, truncated, and capped at 255. The vertices are the mesh's own turned and moved by the actor's matrix, which holds no scale (`CSimpleActor + 0xe4` in the dumps, with the scale apart at `+0x140`), and the normals are read through the client's normal table ([Zone files as the renderer reads them](#zone-files-as-the-renderer-reads-them)) and normalized. No `lights.wld` light in the client has more than one frame.
  - **Checked** against MQPeridotEmu's RoF2 dumps of ten classic zones (Acrylia, the Bastion of Thunder, Cabilis East, Crystal Caverns, Dawnshroud Peaks, Eastern Wastes, Firiona Vie, Grimling Forest, the Halls of Honor, the Plane of Knowledge), which record each actor's share (`+0xc`), first color, and FNV-1a hash of its colors: of 6548 such actors, 6478 hold exactly the share the rule computes, 6482 the same first color, and 6340 every color the same. The floor the client finds for the other 70 (33 in the Halls of Honor, 25 in Crystal Caverns) is not found; for the other 142 some color past the first differs, by how much the dumps do not record.
- **Which lights they take:** a placed static object's model ends up holding colors either way (its own or computed), so it takes only the lights marked for baked geometry (`0x1000e45e` asks the model, `0x10056470`), which `lights.wld` never marks: no `lights.wld` light reaches it. A skeletal one takes every light.

### Lights of radius 0

Four zones' `lights.wld` place seven lights of radius 0 (Runny Eye `L277`, Siren's Grotto `L466`-`L469`, Skyshrine `L1346`, Thurgadin `L244`). The client keeps the radius as stored (`0x1001cd80`, `0x100128f0`), makes its region of influence a sphere of that radius (`0x1000e4f0`), scores it by radius squared over distance squared (`0x1000ff61`), and fades it to nothing at it in every vertex shader, so it lights no vertex. `importZone` leaves them out and names them in `lightsOfRadiusZero`.

## EQ terrain zones

An EQ terrain zone (`EQTZP`, such as the Neighborhood) is a grid of tiles in the zone's `.dat`, each a 17x17 height grid with two vertex colors per vertex and ecosystem layers (`.eco` files). `EQGraphicsDX9.dll` reads the tiles at `0x101004a0` (data versions 20 and 21 differ only in a water sheet block) and draws each ecosystem on a tile as its own pass. The pass's effect is chosen by the ecosystem's texture layers `n` (1-3) and whether its first layer's normal map loaded (`0x1008fe20`, `0x1008ff80`): with it, `SPL\Terrain_Bump<n>Detail.fxo`, technique `TerrainBump<n>Detail_DX9_VS1_PS20`; without it, `Terrain_<n>Detail`, technique `Terrain<n>Detail_DX9_VS1_PS11` (the client logs both in `Logs\dbg.txt`). In this client only Ocean of Tears' `grass_paint` (11 tile passes; its first layer names no normal map) and Crypt of Shade's `grass` (every tile; its normal map is not in the archive) take the second.

**Which files load.** The client opens a loose `<archive>.zon` beside the `.eqg` first (`0x10066230`); one that begins `EQTZP` is the terrain project, loaded from that text (`0x10064bd0`), over any `.zon` the archive holds. A project reads the `.dat` its `*NAME` names (`0x100eb460` reads the name, `0x1010c0d0` opens `<name>.dat`). The one loose terrain file in this client is `oldcommons.zon` (`*NAME commonlands`): Old Commonlands loads it and `commonlands.dat` from `oldcommons.eqg`, whose own `oldcommons.zon` names an `oldcommons.dat` the archive lacks. While the project loads, the terrain loader reads every map, `.lit` file, and detail texture from the zone's archive alone (`0x100a3610` hands it the archive, `0x100cb540` reads from it), so a name the archive lacks finds nothing, even where another archive holds it.

### Vertices

The vertex buffer (`0x100ac170`) holds, per grid vertex:

- **Position:** x and y in whole units, height times 8, each truncated to a short; the shader multiplies the height by 0.125.
- **Normal:** from the heights beside the vertex (`0x100f2190`): `(h(x-1) - h(x+1), h(y-1) - h(y+1), 2 * unitsPerVertex)`, normalized, reaching into the neighboring tile at an edge and one-sided where there is none; packed as color bytes `trunc(255 * (n * 0.5 + 0.5))`.
- **COLOR0:** the tile's second color array: baked light in RGB and the share of scene light in alpha, as on a classic zone (`0xff000000`, black with the full share, by default).
- **COLOR1:** the first color array, a tint (`0x00808080`, neutral, by default).
- **TEXCOORD0:** across the tile's color map and detail mask, from the first texel's center to the last's, in 256ths.
- **TEXCOORD1:** 0 to 1 across the tile, times each detail layer's `DETAILREPEAT` in the shader.

**The drawn triangles.** Each tile's mesh is a binary triangle tree over its quads (`0x100f5c50`, through `0x10106d70`; the game's terrain system mode is 0, `0x100ebe60`, so `0x100ac170` builds the buffer from that tree's triangles). A quad's flag byte sets its kind (`0x100f2100`; read back at `0x100f20c0`): bit `0x01` is kind -1, bit `0x04` kind 1, bit `0x02` kind 0, like no bit. The tree marks both triangles of a kind -1 quad and never emits them (`0x10105a70`, `0x10106020`): such a quad is a hole in the ground, and the tile's collision test skips it too (`0x100cb2b0`). Cave mouths, mine entrances, and gates use them, their own models standing in the hole: 40 zones set the bit, from Ocean of Tears Green Village's 6 quads to Arthicrex's 3430. A kind 1 quad keeps its triangles at full resolution. Elsewhere the tree merges a pair of triangles where the height midway along their long edge lies within the land error, 1.0 (`0x101051d0`), of the average of the edge's ends, splitting neighbors to match (`0x10105f10`); after building it, the client rewrites each quad's diagonal flag `0x80` along the drawn edges (`0x100fdd80`, `0x100f3760`), so the flags saved in the `.dat` describe that merged mesh. The flag picks the quad's diagonal for the height query (`0x100f2fc0`): clear splits it from its (0, 0) corner to (1, 1), set from (1, 0) to (0, 1). The preview draws every quad but the holes, split along its saved diagonal, so where the client merged triangles its ground differs slightly.

### The pass

    light  = COLOR0.rgb + COLOR0.a * (ambient + bounce * max(N . D, 0) + sun * max(N . -D, 0)) + specialAmbient   (clamped to 1)
    detail = mask.r * Detail0(uv1 * repeat0) + mask.g * Detail1(uv1 * repeat1) + mask.b * Detail2(uv1 * repeat2)
    pixel  = 2 * colorMap.rgb * detail * COLOR1.rgb * light,  written with alpha colorMap.a          (Terrain_Bump<n>Detail)
    pixel  = colorMap.rgb * detail * COLOR1.rgb * light,      written with alpha colorMap.a          (Terrain_<n>Detail)

plus point light 0 through the detail normal maps in the bump effects (not drawn). The passes' alphas add to 1 across a tile's ecosystems. `Terrain1Detail_DX9_VS1_PS11` pairs the vertex shader at `0x1fa8` of `Terrain_1Detail.fxo` (light in `oD0`, the tint unchanged in `oD1`) with the pixel shader at `0x1e80` (`tex t0; tex t1; mul r0.xyz, t1, t0; mul r0.xyz, r0, v1; mul r0.xyz, r0, v0`), so it does not double the tint; the vertex shader that doubles light times tint (`0x1200`) belongs to the fixed-function `DX6_VS1_PS0` technique (which pass uses which shader: the effect's resource table after its techniques).

A detail map the archive lacks leaves its layer's texture empty (`0x100edb10` through `0x100e9fb0`; the layer starts with none, `0x1009f9f0`), and the pass binds no texture to that sampler (`0x10089040`, `0x100890dc`). Direct3D 9 reads a sampler without a texture as 0, 0, 0, 1 ([texldl](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3dhlsl/texldl---ps)), so that layer's share of the detail is black: in Crypt of Shade, whose every tile has `grass` without its detail map, the ground draws black wherever no later ecosystem covers it; in Valdeholm, `2ndll`'s `RiverB` layer (heights 151 to 152) adds nothing. The DX9 techniques read no fallback detail map; only the fixed-function DX6 ones read `DetailColorMapFallback`.

### Tile textures

Each ecosystem on a tile has a 32x32 color map and detail mask (A8R8G8B8, no mipmaps). The terrain system's distance table (`0x100ec12a`) gives 32 texels at every distance. They are computed once per tile (`0x100f3fd0`):

- **Height and slope per texel:** texel `t` samples the vertex grid at `t * 16 / 31` along each axis, bilinearly: the height, and the normal's z, whose slope in degrees comes from a table, `acos(round(z * 1000) / 1000)` (`0x100ec630`).
- **Layer weights (`0x100ee790`):** a texture layer's factor is 1 inside `MINHEIGHT`-`MAXHEIGHT` and `MINSLOPE`-`MAXSLOPE`, falling off linearly across `HEIGHTTOL` and `SLOPETOL` outside them (the two factors multiplied), and the texel is left out beyond the tolerances. Working from the last layer back to the second, each takes its factor of what later ones left, `trunc(factor * (255 - taken))` (the FPU set to truncate, `0x100eecee`-`0x100eed1d`), the bytes wrapping; the first layer takes the rest (`0x100eeed0`). A layer's own defaults (`0x100edde0`) are heights -10000 to 10000 with tolerance 10, slopes 0 to 90 with tolerance 1, softness 0, layering area 16.
- **Blend map (`0x100eec0d`-`0x100eecbe`):** a layer's `BLENDMAP`, when it loads (`0x100eda30`, 8-bit maps only), turns its falloff into a threshold on the map's values, read at 32x32 from the map's box-filtered mip chain (`0x100a0410`, `0x100a02d0`), so it repeats once per tile. Only where the factor is strictly between 0.02 and 0.98: `t = trunc((1 - factor) * 255)`, `threshold = trunc(0.01 * t * t - 255)` (0.01 as a double), `slope = trunc(t * (100 - BLENDSOFTNESS) * 0.001 + 1)` (the product rounded to single precision first), and `value = (blend - threshold) * slope`; a negative value leaves the texel out, one over 255 makes the factor 1, and otherwise the factor becomes `value / 255` (in the client's single-precision 1/255). The soft falloff becomes a ragged edge along the blend map's grain. Eleven zones use them (Arcstone's `blend.bmp`, the others `grassyblend.bmp`).
- **Layering map (`0x100ee8c1`-`0x100ee981`, `0x100eeadb`-`0x100eec0b`):** spans `LAYERINGAREA` tiles in each direction (the tile's window at its longitude and latitude modulo the area); a value of 0 leaves the texel out and one below 255 scales the factor by it over 255; a map read past `*LAYERINGMAPINPUTSIZE` is read at its own size and interpolated. No zone in this client ships one: Commonlands and Old Commonlands name `blend-grayscale.bmp`, which their archives lack, and a map that does not load is not applied (`0x100edca0`). The preview refuses a layering map that loads.
- **A map that does not load:** the terrain's bitmap lookup (`0x100ea060`) falls back to the system's default bitmap, which only the terrain editor's mode loads (`0x100eb170`; the game's stays empty, `0x100ebe60`), so a cover, blend, or layering map the archive lacks is no map at all.
- **Color map RGB (`0x100eeed0`):** each layer's `COVERMAP` times its weight / 255, summed: a weight below 3 adds nothing and one of 253 or more takes the cover map whole. The cover map is read at 32x32 from the mip chain D3DX builds for it with a box filter (the cover maps ship without mips), so it repeats once per tile. A later layer whose cover map did not load adds no color, though its weight still takes its share: Atiiki's `ro_sand` lacks `sand_grassy`'s `coverage_desert.dds`, so its high flats draw darker. Without the first layer's cover map the client writes no color at all, leaving whatever the texture held; no zone does that, and the preview refuses it.
- **Coverage, the color map's alpha (`0x100f4690`):** each later ecosystem's 64x64 mask from the `.dat` (texel `t` reads mask texel `2t`) times each layer's weight, scaled by 1/65535 and summed. Working from the last ecosystem back to the second, each takes that share of what later ones left, snapped to 0 below 3 and 255 from 253. The first ecosystem takes the rest.
- **Detail mask (`0x100ac770`):** red, green, and blue are the first three layers' weights. A texel on the tile's edge is averaged with the neighboring tile's texel beside it where that tile has the same ecosystem (left, right, below, above, in that order).

Child layers (`*CHILDLAYER`) are weighed under their parent (`0x100ee790`); no zone uses them, and the preview refuses them.

### Objects

- **Placements:** a tile's placements give a model (`.mod`, with `.lod` levels the client switches by distance), the ecosystem that placed it, a tile and an offset from that tile's origin, turns in degrees, and a scale. The client makes the object at the tile and offset with its z kept apart (`0x101009b1`-`0x10100a66`); when the tile that lists it starts (`0x100f3a60`), it reads the ground for each of its objects where the offset falls once wrapped into a tile (`WorldToTile`, `0x100eb280`: the offset's remainder by the tile size, a tile size added when it is negative) from the listing tile's own heights (`0x100f2a30`, `0x100f2fc0`, which reads 0 off the tile), and stands the object that far up plus its z (`0x100f8ac0`). So an object whose offset runs past its tile's edge takes the ground of its own tile's far side: Elddar Forest's two bridges stand about 45 and 73 units over the ground under them, spanning its ravines; Steppes' four cave boxes, listed by tiles whose offsets run off the map, stand where no tile is. EQEmu's `azone2` (`utils/deprecated/azone2/dat.cpp`) found the same by hand-editing zone files. Most placements have z 0; hand-placed rocks sink a few units.
- **Object groups (`.tog`):** a group's z is a height in the world, not above the ground: its members are placed from it unchanged (`TileToWorld`, `0x100eb380`), and no grounding pass reaches them (`0x100f2a30`, `0x100f2ac0`, and `0x100f2ba0` walk the tile's objects, areas, and lights). Each member stands at the group's position plus its offset turned by the group's turns (`0x100a75c0`) and scaled by the group's x scale, raised by the group's tenth value times that scale; it turns by its own turns plus the group's, axis by axis (wrapped to 0-360), and scales by its scale times the group's on each axis (`0x101038c0`). Across the 864 groups standing on a tile, z lies a median 2 units from the ground under it (and is a median 80 from 0). The tenth value lifts Sunderock's land bridges 56 to 156 units onto the canyon rims and sinks the mouth of Steamfont's third cave 75 units into its hole.
- **Baked light of group members:** each member names a `.lit` file (a count, then a D3DCOLOR per vertex). The client copies the count of colors from the loader's buffer (`0x100a5810`), and the model takes them only when the count equals its vertices (`0x100548d0` logs "LIT data has %d vertices, expected it to have %d vertices for %s, so LIT data is being ignored!"), so the Neighborhood's zone-out wall, whose `.lit` holds 2175 colors for 1788 vertices, draws without it. A `.lit` the archive lacks sets no light data at all (`0x10053aeb`): 46 files in 9 zones, among them Commonlands' plague hut lamps, barrels, and rug and Fungal Forest's fungi. A `.lit` holding fewer colors than its count is copied past its end from the loader's pooled buffer (`0x100cb540`: the file, a 0 byte, then whatever an earlier file left there), so the colors past its end are undefined; in this client every such file's count matches its model (Blacksail's and the Buried Sea's `sharpeye_pal` masts, 286 of 511 colors; the Buried Sea's `whp_obj_whp_combine_ruins`, 49 of 176; Steamfont's `akanon_entry_obj_pipe_outera09`, none of 175). The preview draws all three kinds without baked light, as objects without baked light draw, and the import lists them (`litFilesMissing`, `litFilesNotMatchingModels`, `litFilesShorterThanTheirCount`).

Neighborhood calibration: the two day shots redraw within about 13.5 and 16.6 levels of 255, using the zone's header fog and the doors, ground items, and NPCs of the bristle behavior recording at each shot's moment. The Peridot server's door table agrees with the live dump for the gate in the second shot (`OBJ_GUILDGATE`, door 134). NPC equipment, radial flora (grass cards near the camera), the sky, and level-of-detail models are not drawn yet. The group heights above change nothing in them (the two groups the Neighborhood draws stand at z 0 on ground at 0); the layer weights were rounded when they were measured, where they now truncate, a difference of at most one 255th of a weight.

Not settled, and what would settle it:

- **The empty sampler:** that Direct3D 9 reads a sampler without a texture as black comes from Microsoft's documentation, not from the client on this hardware. A RoF2 screenshot in Crypt of Shade (`cryptofshade`, entered directly after starting the client, so no Hills of Shade terrain is cached), anywhere in the open at midday, eye height, level, would show the ground black, as the preview draws it, or textured.
- **Baked light read past a file's end:** the colors are whatever the loader's buffer held; the preview draws those members without baked light. RoF2 screenshots of Blacksail's palisade at (4647, 637), its `sharpeye_pal` masts at eye height facing them at midday, and of Steamfont's Ak'Anon entrance pipes at (-1756, 535), would show what the client draws there.

- **Tilt order:** the order of a placement's two tilts, and so the turns of the 16 tilted groups' members, follows the actor's rotation matrix (`0x100c1c20`), not traced; a RoF2 screenshot of Sunderock's land bridge at (-2545, 0), from (-2200, 0) at eye height facing west, would show its 3-degree tilt.

## EQG zones

An EQG zone is an `.eqg` archive whose `.zon` (EQGZ) places one `.ter` terrain mesh and many `.mod` objects. This is the export format. The import that draws client EQG zones is the first item of the [worklist](renderingWorklist.md). What is traced so far:

- **Which `.zon` loads (`0x10066230`):** the client first opens a loose `<archive>.zon` beside the `.eqg`. If it exists, that file is the zone: version 2 (`0x10065430`), or an EQ terrain zone. Otherwise the archive's own `.zon` loads, which must be version 1 (`0x10064da0`). In this client, all 166 loose `.zon` files are version 2. Of the 37 inside archives, 36 are version 1; the one version 2 file inside an archive cannot load that way. The RoF2 client confirms this for the Guild Lobby (below).
- **Baked light:** a version 1 placement takes it from `<placement name>.lit` in the archive (magic `EQGP`, a count, then a D3DCOLOR per vertex). A version 2 placement carries it inline. Either way it reaches the model instance (`0x100548d0`), which takes it only when its count equals the model's vertex count and otherwise logs "LIT data ... is being ignored!" to the client's `Logs\dbg.txt`. MQPeridotEmu's dump records each actor's baked light: its count, its first color, and a 32-bit FNV-1a hash of its colors' bytes, which a file's colors can be checked against.
- **A `.lit` shorter than its count:** the version 1 loader (`0x10064da0`) reads the header's count, allocates that many colors from the zone's memory pool (`0x10012fd0`, whose blocks come from `operator new` uncleared, `0x10012f00`), and reads that many through the archive, whose read stops at the file's end (`0x100db120`). Delve (`delvea`) has 17 such files, for `obj_doorcaps`, `obp_dragvert`, and `obp_dragvert_` placements: each counts its model's vertices (1152, 391, 356) and holds 46 to 98 colors, so the client takes the held colors and, past them, whatever the pool held. The import draws the held colors and no baked light past them (no baked light, the full share of scene light) and counts those placements by model in `bakedLightPastFileEnd`. Not settled: what the client shows past the held colors, which can differ between sessions; an MQPeridotEmu dump in Delve (each `OBJ_DOORCAPS` and `OBP_DRAGVERT` actor's hash and first color) with a screenshot of a door cap would show whether the rest is zero (black, taking no scene light) there.
- **Skinned (boned) models a zone places:** a `.mod` that stores bones becomes an `_HMD` model with a hierarchical actor definition (`0x100624e0`). The version 1 and 2 loaders (`0x10064da0`, `0x10065430`) and the EQ terrain object group loader (`0x100a5810`) make its placement a `CHierarchicalActor` without baked light (the group loader reads no `.lit` for it), so it takes every point light and draws with the `SkinMesh` effects, which read no vertex color: ambient, bounce, sun, and point lights on normals blended through the bones' inverse transposes (`SkinMeshCB1.fxo`). The actor looks up `<model>_DEFAULT` (`0x10044550`, `0x10044bd0`) and loops it from `rand() / 32767` of its length (`0x1011060e` is the C runtime's `rand`), so each placement stands at an unpredictable point of its loop; the preview draws every placement at the animation's first key, or in the bind pose where the zone registers no such animation. As the DLL loads an animation it lowers `ROOT_BONE`'s translation keys by the `moddat.ini` `ROffset` of the section named by the animation name's last three characters (`0x1003c960`; not for names holding `_MT_` or whose first `_IT` is followed by a digit), 3.125 where that section has none (`0x10137f94`): every `_DEFAULT` animation reads `[ULT]`, which `moddat.ini` lacks, so these models stand 3.125 below their placements. The client's zones place 10 such models in 12 EQG zone variants (`obj_gearfurnacea`, `obj_ceilingfan`, `obj_cntrl_box`, `obp_flg_`, `obp_pirateflag`, ...) and `obj_gears` and `obp_flag_` in the object groups of three EQ terrain zones, each with its `_default.ani` in its archive. Their texture coordinates are read unflipped, as for every skinned model (Texture orientation). The import counts them by model in `animatedModels` with the animation that poses them. Not settled: the drop and the pose in game; a RoF2 screenshot in Steamfactory or Guardian of the Gears close beside a gear furnace (`obj_gearfurnacea`), with its dump for the camera, would show whether it stands 3.125 below its placement, and a second taken a moment later that it moves.
- **The Guild Lobby in the RoF2 client** (MQPeridotEmu's dump with `dbg.txt`): the client loads the loose version 2 `.zon`. It places 63 candle torches as that file does, not the archive's 64, and none of the archive-only models; the 11 models no archive holds (`obj_aframe.mod`, `obj_window.mod`, `obp_streetlamp.mod`, ...) are not drawn. The 133 objects whose inline colors fit draw with exactly those colors. The terrain's inline colors (57912 for 61096 vertices) and those of the other 116 objects are ignored, and those objects end up with colors the client computes per placement: every one has alpha `0x19`, where colors taken from the file have alpha `0x00`. `AFRAME_`, `GUILD_DOOR_` and `TRANS_ENTRY` are the server's doors, not zone objects.
- **Terrain stands where its vertices are:** the `.zon` places its `.ter` turned and moved (most zones a quarter turn, -pi/2, and an offset), but the client draws the terrain at its own vertices and the placement goes unused. Measured in Broodlands, Crescent Reach, Freeport East, Highpass Hold, and the Arena: only so do the trees, posts, crates, and barrels placed on the terrain stand on it (Crescent Reach: 232 of 241 at no gap; with the placement applied, none), and RoF2 screenshots of Highpass Hold with MQPeridotEmu's camera match the import only so. Export places its terrain at the origin unturned, which is the same either way.
- **Placements:** model names lose their extension and take `_ACTORDEF`. Rotations are radians, turned into 512ths of a turn as (field 1, -field 2, field 3) times 512 / 2pi. A light's position reads as (field 2, -field 1, field 3) (`0x10065acb`): only so do Freeport West's 24 `LIB_toch_main` lights stand on its torch models, 3.5 units over each.
- **Effects:** a material's shader name selects the effect family by mesh kind: `Opaque_MaxCB1.fx` draws zone terrain with `SPL\RegionCB1`, placed objects with `SModelCB1`, and skinned models with `SkinMeshCB1`. `RegionCB1`'s vertex shader lights each vertex exactly as `RegionOldA` does (baked RGB plus share times scene light, special ambient, and three point lights). Its normal map adds only point light 0.
- **Regions:** each is a name (its prefix says what it is: `AWT_` water, `ALV_` lava, `ATP_` zone line, `ASL_` ice, `APV`, `APK`) and nine floats: a box's center, three turns, and its half extents, the center in the axes the terrain's vertices use. Some files store a half extent negative; its size is the same. The first turn is a heading in 512ths of a turn, counter-clockwise from above, about the box's center. Files write it in one of two units, both meant as 512ths when read so: in the 30 EQG zone variants whose values run past 2 pi (most -128, a quarter turn, or 256, a half), reading them as 512ths puts more of the zone's water surface inside its `AWT_` boxes than leaving them unturned in 20 (Argath 61% to 97%, Rubak 75% to 100%, Sepulcher 77% to 99%) and less in none by over 1%, and in Mechanotus every box of one shape then covers the same water; in the 31 written in radians (-pi/2 in most), the value turns its boxes about a degree, which changes no zone's share by more than about 1%, while reading those values as radians lowers it in 19 (Freeport Temple 98% to 32%), and Crescent Reach's narrow river boxes, each 22.75 wide along x and stepping 22.75 along it, tile edge to edge only unturned. The other two turns, set on a few boxes, are untraced: the survey gives them as stored, and imports place no box with either set. The zone lines this reading gives sit across the passages out of the zone: Crescent Reach's one spans its eastern canyon wall to wall, and Dranik's Scar's three stand on the two tubes and the trench leading off its terrain, each beside Brewall's "to" label for it. Export writes axis-aligned boxes with the turns 0, which reads the same either way. Whether the client applies the turns is not yet checked in game (swimming at a box's edge would show it).
- **Water** (`Opaque_MaxWater.fx`, terrain `SPL\RegionWater`, objects `SModelWater`; DX9 technique `ps_2_0`): the diffuse is not read. The color runs from `e_fWaterColor1` seen from straight above to `e_fWaterColor2` at grazing angles (by 1 - view·normal), lit by the vertex light like any surface, plus point light 0 by the normal. The normal is the normal map sampled at the texture coordinates and at twice them, each scrolled by a slide times time, summed, and flattened with distance until flat 300 units off. The environment cube map, looked up along the reflected view ray, adds `e_fReflectionAmount` times `e_fReflectionColor` times fresnel: `e_fFresnelBias` plus the rest times the grazing term to `e_fFresnelPower` (the constants holding 1 - bias and the color difference come from the effect's preshader and are taken to be those). Alpha is 2.9 times fresnel. The texture coordinates are read as shorts over 256, so the normal map repeats as often as the coordinates do; `e_fUVScale` defaults to 1 and no client material sets it. The effect's defaults match `Resources\WaterSwap\WaterSwap.ini`'s new water (fresnel bias 0.25, power 8, reflection 0.7). Highpass Hold's water drawn this way matches RoF2 screenshots in hue and ripple size. The preview draws it opaque, without point light 0, and with the environment cube map's average color in place of its lookup (worklist item 11); against RoF2 shots of Freeport East's harbor (`EQ000059`, `EQ000060`) that draws the distant water turquoise where the client's is deep blue and the near water opaque where the client's shows the bottom. The import counts water triangles in `drawnOtherwise` (`waterOpaque`), and lava, drawn as its two diffuses averaged, in `lavaDiffusesAveraged`.
- **Waterfalls** (`Opaque_MaxWaterFall.fx`, `SPL\RegionWaterFall`, DX8 technique `ps_1_1`): the diffuse at the texture coordinates scrolled by the first slide, times the vertex light, is the color; the diffuse's alpha at the coordinates scrolled by the second slide is the alpha, so a fall is as see-through as its texture.
- **MPL materials:** most of the client's EQG geometry is drawn with the `MPL` shader family (`Opaque_MPLBasic`, `MPLBump`, `MPLBump2UV`, `MPLGB`, `MPLSB`, `MPLRB`, `MPLFull`, ..., the names `0x10014660` tests for): about 24.9 million of the 36.2 million triangles of the client's `.eqg` materials, against 11.3 million in the `Max` family; 162 of the 200 EQG zone archives with a terrain draw more than three quarters of their triangles with them, among them Freeport West (all but 0.2%) and Highpass Hold (98%). Under the single-pass path they draw with `SPL\Region_Bump`, `SModel_Bump`, and their `_2UV` and other variants (the dumps list each one's current technique, such as `RegionBump_DX8_VS1_PS11` and `SModelBump2UV_DX8_VS1_PS11`). Their DX8 technique lights each vertex as `RegionCB1` does but for point light 0 (`Region_Bump.fxo` vertex shader at `0x1d64`): baked RGB plus the share times ambient, bounce, and sun, plus special ambient and point lights 1 and 2, with point light 0's falloff in the alpha; the pixel shader (`0x1b88`) adds point light 0 through the normal map and multiplies by saturate(diffuse times coverage) times `COLOR1` times `Tint`. The coverage is the material's `e_TextureCoverage0`, read at the texture coordinates times `e_fCoverageScale0` (`MPLBump`) or at the second texture coordinates (`MPLBump2UV`, `SModel_Bump_2UV.fxo` `0x1fb8`); `COLOR1` is the vertex buffer's second color (version 3 models store a vertex color of their own: Freeport West's buildings `0xFF808080`, its terrain mostly gray from `0x78` to `0xFF` and some near black). The preview draws these materials by their diffuse alone, so Freeport West draws lighter than RoF2 by day and night: in the shots its walls come out at about half to two thirds of the light the preview gives them, while Highpass Hold, calibrated at noon within about 17 levels, shows how much the difference varies with the coverage maps and vertex colors. The courtyard beyond the gate passage, whose terrain vertices around (-25, -537) carry second colors `0x1F1F1F` and `0x0B0C09`, draws near black in the noon shot (`EQ000014`) where the preview draws it lit, as a second color multiplied in would. What the draw puts in `COLOR1` and `Tint` is not traced (taken literally, `0x80` gray times the coverage maps' means of about 0.4 would draw darker than the shots), nor which variants the other `MPL` names take; worklist item 1. The import counts the triangles drawn so in `drawnOtherwise` (`mplByDiffuseAlone`), with `AddAlpha` materials drawn opaque (`additiveDrawnOpaque`, worklist item 5).
- **Freeport West in the RoF2 client** (MQPeridotEmu's dumps of 2026-10-04 at the gate and smithy cameras): of the loose `.zon`'s 2323 placements, every one whose inline colors fit its model carries exactly those colors (2268 actors, each hash matching its file's), the 50 whose colors do not fit (carts, tents, fire pits) carry none, and the terrain's 125884 colors fit its vertices. The import reads and draws them so. Many of its walls carry no baked light in the file: 961 placements are all `0xFF000000` (no baked light, the full share of scene light), among them `OBJ_smithy_exterior00`, and the gate passage `OBP_wall_int_passageA_00` holds `0x40000000` (a quarter share) on its brick faces. So the warm light on those walls at night in RoF2, where the preview drew them black, is not baked light: it is the character's carried light ([Point lights](#point-lights)).
- **Textures no linked archive holds** (47 in 35 EQG and EQ terrain zones; 17 zones' `FailsafeShader` materials name `grid_standard.dds`, which none ships): the effect's sampler is left without a texture, which Direct3D 9 reads as 0, 0, 0, 1 ([texldl](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3dhlsl/texldl---ps)), so the faces draw black, lit and fogged, as the terrain's missing detail maps do ([EQ terrain zones](#eq-terrain-zones)). That the model loader binds no stand-in texture is not traced. A RoF2 screenshot close beside Freeport West's `OBJ_cart_00` at (-854.1, -269.8, -150.3), whose 2 of 1,499 triangles name `grid_standard.dds`, or Freeport East's `OBJ_office_drawer01` knobs at (-1037.6, 719.2, 30.7), each with its dump, would settle it. The import lists them in `missingTextures`.
- **Objects whose baked light doesn't fit:** in Freeport West's dumps the 50 hold no colors; in the Guild Lobby's (the same MQPeridotEmu 0.2) the 116 hold as many colors as their models have vertices, every one of alpha `0x19`, which the client made itself. When and how it makes them is not traced (the Lobby's hashes and first colors are what a trace can be checked against); they look like the load-time light a classic zone's placed objects get ([Classic zones](#placements-and-their-vertex-light)), given to a static actor whose model holds no colors once the zone has loaded, the alpha `0x19` the share of the floor beneath, which is not checked for EQG placements. The preview draws both kinds with no baked light and the full share of scene light. `0x1009d670`, which folds the scene's ambient sources into one constant for an object without baked light, belongs to the lit batches path (`0x10055810`, lighting on the CPU), which only WLD objects take: a WLD model's definition copies `LitBatches: Enabled` into the flag that picks it (`0x10056539`), an EQG model's (`_SMD`, `0x10056670`) clears it (`0x1005669e`), so an EQG object draws with its `SModel` effect. Where a version 1 or 2 model stores no vertex color, its vertices carry `0xFF808080` (`0x100624e0`); how the draw feeds an object without baked light to the effect's `COLOR0` is not traced, and the preview draws it with no baked light and the full share of scene light.

## Point lights

Traced in `EQGraphicsDX9.dll`, `eqgame.exe`, and the client's compiled effects, and checked in pictures and against RoF2 night shots of Freeport West and the Plane of Knowledge (below).

- **The falloff:** every SPL vertex shader adds, for each of three point lights, `color * clamp(N . L / |L|, 0, 1) * (1 - min((|L| * k)^2, 1))`, unscaled by the vertex's share of scene light, before the clamp to 1. Its preshader computes `k = rcp(PointLight<i>Color.w)` (`RegionCB1.fxo`: `c5`, `c6`, `c7`, FXLC op `0x103`), and the DLL puts the light's radius in that `w` (`0x10089ec7`-`0x1008a0c2`, in `0x10089a60`): a light reaches exactly its radius and gives half its color at 0.707 of it. An empty slot gets color 0, `w` 0.01 (`0x10131fd4`), and a position 10000 below. `SModel*` (placed objects) does the same through the world and inverse-transpose matrices, then multiplies by the instance tint; it does not renormalize the turned normal.
- **Fixed function and multipass:** the fixed-function path sets a `D3DLIGHT9` point light with `Attenuation2 = 20 / r^2` (`0x10131fcc`), and the multipass path (`0x10099fc0`) light 0 at half its color; RoF2 with shaders and `MultiPassLighting=0` takes neither.
- **Which lights a draw gets (`0x1000fd90`):** up to three of the lights whose dPVS region of influence (a sphere of the light's radius, `0x1000e4f0`) is active for the draw, by `factor * 2 * lightness * r^2 / d^2` (`0x1000ff61`), highest first; a selection sort keeps the earlier of equal scores (`0x1000ffe2`). Lightness is `(max(rgb) + min(rgb)) / 2`; `d` is measured from the draw's center: a region's bounding box center, an actor's position. The factor is 100 or 10 for lights flagged at `+0x1d` or `+0x1c` (not zone lights), 1 otherwise.
- **LIB_ and LIT_ (`0x1000ff3c`):** zone regions (`0x1000db20`, `0x1000dc10`) and actors whose model keeps baked light (`0x10056470`: the colors `0x100548d0` keeps when their count fits) take only lights flagged at `+0x64`; other actors, characters among them, take every light. The EQG zone loaders flag a light whose name's third character is `B` (`LIB_`, against `LIT_`; version 1 `0x100653e0`, version 2 `0x10065b13`); `lights.wld` lights are never flagged (`0x1001cf69`). Every light `eqgame.exe` makes through the scene graph (vtable `0x70`, `0x1006ace0`) is flagged, carried lights among them. So in an EQG zone a `LIT_` light lights only characters and objects without baked light (its light on baked geometry is already in the baked colors), and the few `LIB_` lights light the baked geometry too: across 202 EQG zone variants, 24030 `LIT_` lights and 1593 `LIB_`. ## Particle emitters

Traced in `EQGraphicsDX9.dll` (the particle code) and `eqgame.exe` (the emitter list), and checked in pictures and against RoF2 screenshots of Highpass Hold's waterfall.

- **Definitions (`.edd`):** `"EDD\0"`, the version `"110\0"`, then 416-byte records read whole (`0x10077519`-`0x10077549`); record `i` is definition `i` (record 0, `None`, names the texture `nul`). The particle system keeps three collections: `SpellsNew.edd` (`+0x14`), `EnvironmentEmittersNew.edd` (`+0x5c`), and `ActorEmittersNew.edd` (`+0x80`); a zone's emitter list indexes the environment collection, 626 definitions in this client. A definition's texture is looked for in `SpellEffects`, `EnvEmitterEffects`, and `ActorEffects`, in that order (`0x10071d18`). `eqEmitterDefinitions` names the fields the particle code reads; it reads no others.
- **The emitter list** (`eqgame.exe` `0x4a1c30`, `0x4a1e00`): fields by position ([assetCatalog.md](assetCatalog.md#lights-and-emitters-in-blender)). `0x4a1ba0` hands every line to the scene graph's emitter creation (`EQGraphicsDX9.dll` `0x1006af00`, its vtable's `0x80`) and ignores what it returns; that makes no emitter for a definition index below 0 or at or past the environment collection's count, or for a lifespan of 0 or less, checked in that order. Discord Tower's list gives `brazier35`, `brazier32`, and `brazier02` the indices -7, -101, and -497 (and lifespan 0); 262 lines across the client's lists have a lifespan of 0 and 4 an index past the 626 definitions. An import places every line as an emitter and groups those the client makes none for in `emittersNotMade`; previews draw none of them.
- **How many and when** (`0x1006c9ed`, the emitter constructor `0x1006c687`): a definition spawns `perEvent` particles at `eventRate` events a second after `startDelay`, each living `particleLife` seconds, into slots for every particle alive at once (`capacity` when given). Beyond `lodDistance` from the camera an emitter keeps `lodDistance / distance` of its particles, never fewer than 16 of its steady count.
- **Where they start** (`0x10073204`): shapes 0-9 (point, even ring, even cylinder in rows `0x10072c5f`, cylinder, disc, sphere, box `0x100737e4`, cone, torus, ring) in cylinder coordinates about the emitter's axis A (up), turned toward B (+x) and C (+y) by `tiltB` and `tiltC` in 512ths of a turn (`0x10072fe0`) after `offsetA`-`offsetC` move the center (`0x10072f14`). A definition marked `emitterScaled` takes the emitter's scale, which an environment emitter passes as 0 (`0x1006af68` through `0x100705e0`), so its particles all start at the emitter.
- **How they move:** along A by `velocityA` (random between its two bounds) and `accelerationA`, outward by `velocityOut` and `accelerationOut`, along B and C by theirs, around A by `swirl`, down by `gravity` (`g t^2 / 2`), and along +x by `driftX t`. A particle whose radius crosses the axis is dropped unless `crossesAxis` (`0x10074810`).
- **How they look:** opacity fades in over `alphaFadeIn` and out over `alphaFadeOut`, at most `maximumAlpha`; size `sizeScale` fades in over `sizeFadeIn` and out over `sizeFadeOut`, and height and width shrink over `heightFadeOut` and `widthFadeOut` (`0x1007224d`-`0x1007231e`). Height and width are random within their bounds, one draw for both with `linkedSize` (`0x10072eb9`-`0x10072f11`). Color runs from the start bytes to the end bytes over the particle's life. The texture frame is `framesPerSecond * age`, rounded half to even by the FPU (`0x100754de`), in a 2x2, 4x2, or 4x4 grid of cells.
- **Billboards:** mode 0 square to the view, turned by the particle's spin; mode 1 a beam its height long along A, turned toward the camera (`0x1007499d`); mode 2 lying in the world's level plane, turned by the spin, its corners along `(cos, sin, 0)` and `(sin, -cos, 0)` put through the view rotation (`0x10074d5f`): water rings, splashes, and glows on the ground. Billboards turned to their screen velocity (`velocityAligned`) are not drawn; no client zone places one.
- **The draw** (`0x10071b90`, state block 5 at `0x1009f2b8`): source alpha over inverse source alpha, or added (destination ONE) when `additive` (`0x10071ebe`); never fogged (`0x1009ed1b`); without depth writes when `noDepthWrite`. The frame (`0x10097420`) draws the scene, its six passes from Opaque to AddAlpha (`0x1008b470`, their lists freed at `0x1008c550`), before it moves and draws the particles (`0x100759a0`, `0x10072110`), so particles blend over every zone surface, blended ones included.
- **Clipping:** a particle deeper into the view than 500 units, less its `depthBias`, is not drawn (`0x100721e0`, `0x10074987`), nor one nearer than its collection's near clip plane (`eqclient.ini`'s `EnvironmentParticleNearClipPlane`, 8 here, which `eqgame.exe` hands the engine at `0x49cb2a`; `0x10074973`); an emitter more than 10 units inside that plane is skipped whole (`0x10073f4f`).
- **In the preview** (`emitterParticles`, `bridgeEmitterDrawing`, `emitterAssets`): a client-shaded view draws each emitter's particles as they stand at one moment of its steady state. The client's random draws come from a generator seeded by the emitter's name and position, so a view draws the same particles every time. Particles draw after the zone's blended surfaces, unfogged, none deeper than 500. The server reads the definitions and makes readable copies of their textures once under the tooling root (`emitters\`).
- **Checked:** Highpass Hold's waterfall at (-668, 479), drawn from the cameras of two midday RoF2 screenshots (MQPeridotEmu dumps at 13:06:17 and 13:06:57 on 2026-10-03), stands where the screenshots show it; the `geyserbottom` mist emitter at its foot draws as a white cloud.

Not settled, and what would settle it:

- **The near clip plane's units:** the DLL compares the collection's value with view depth, but the engine's setter is not traced, so the preview clips no near particles. A RoF2 screenshot standing a few units from a torch flame (Freeport West, beside `LIB_toch_main04` at (12.7, -523.5), facing it, any time of day) would show whether flames within 8 units vanish.
- **The far clip's other operand:** the 500 cap is a minimum with a view value (`+0x24`) not traced; the preview draws every particle within 500.
- **Draw order between emitters:** the client draws each collection's definitions and emitters in its own order, not by distance; the preview sorts emitters and particles far to near. Overlapping blended (not added) particles of two emitters could layer differently.
- **Blended zone surfaces and depth:** whether the client's waterfall and water surfaces write depth decides whether particles behind them show; the preview draws them dithered, writing depth, so mist behind a fall is hidden. In the RoF2 screenshots the fall's foot glows white across the fall where the preview shows mist only beside it. Two RoF2 screenshots from the camera of the 13:06:57 dump (eye (-560, 300, -36.3), heading 168.7 and pitch -10.9 in 512ths) at midday, one with `EnvironmentParticleDensity` 0 and one at 1, would show what the mist adds and whether it shows through the fall.
- **Density and opacity options:** `EnvironmentParticleDensity` and `EnvironmentParticleOpacity` (1 here) reach the engine untraced; the preview draws at full density and opacity.
- **The field at `0x170`:** the particle code reads it, but its role is not traced, so it is not used.

## Sky

Traced in `EQGraphicsDX9.dll` and `eqgame.exe` and checked against a RoF2 screenshot of Highpass Hold at midday with MQPeridotEmu's dump. `eqSky` reads the client's sky files and `skyDrawing` draws them; previews draw the zone's sky (`setZoneProperties` `sky`) as the world behind everything.

- **Which sky:** the sky type is the zone's short name (the zone header's override when it has one; EQEmu sends none). A type `sky.ini` does not define falls back to `default`, without a log line: Highpass Hold, the guild lobby and hall, and any new zone get `default`, whose weather is `DefaultClear`. The sky draws only when the player's Sky option is on, the header's sky byte is not 0, and, with fog on, the fog end drawn (after the pull-in) is at least 101.
- **Files** (`Resources\Sky`): `sky.ini` holds the types (`[SkySetting-<name>]`: DefaultWeather and the horizon band's angles), `weather.ini` the weathers and what they use. An object loads only when its kind's index section lists it and its own `[Kind-Name]` section exists; names ignore case, and the first of a repeated section or key wins. A weather names a color set, a sun and a moon, clouds, and the weathers it turns to in storms.
- **Time:** the hour and minute give `t = (hour * 60 + minute) / 1440` of a day. Color sets and satellites list nodes by time; the last node started is in effect (before the first, the last), and inside a node's transition it fades from the node before by `(new * w + old * (255 - w)) >> 8`, `w = 255 * elapsed / transition`, times in 65536ths of a day. The midday screenshot, taken after the server's `#set time 12`, matches `t` at 13:00 (mean difference 3.3 levels of 255 against 26.4 at 12:00).
- **Color map** (32 x 32): rows 0-29 of column 0 color the dome by angle from the sun, row 30 is the horizon band's top color, and column 31 holds the scene's light: row 0 the sun, 1 the moon, 2 the fog color, 3 the ambient, 28 the sun's bounce, 29 the moon's. The color map's alpha is the stars' visibility.
- **What the sky sets:** with a sky drawn, `eqgame.exe` replaces the zone header's fog color with the sky's (`0x48ae80`); fog start, end, and density stay the header's. The ambient is the sky's (`0x4944b4`), raised to the viewer's floor ([Where the lighting values come from](#where-the-lighting-values-come-from)). Each frame (`0x496af0`) the directional light takes the sun's color (row 0) and bounce (row 28), or the moon's (rows 1 and 29) when CSky says the moon lights the scene: before `t` 0.24652778 and after 0.76388890 (`EQGraphicsDX9.dll` `0x100c20a0`, single-precision constants `0x1013f984` and `0x1013f988`), 5:55 and 18:20, with `t` taken as minutes times 1/1440 in single precision (`0x496b8d`). From 5:55 to 6:00 and from 18:00 to 18:20 the sun lights the scene from below the horizon. The direction is `(0, sin(512 - a), -cos(512 - a))` in the client's 512-step tables (`0x496bc7`-`0x496c33`; sine at `0x100b92e0`, cosine at `0x100b9340`, built at `0x100b9680`), the direction the light travels, with `a` the sun's angle, or the moon's, in 512ths of a turn. `eqgame.exe` sets the sun's angle from its clock (`0x496d80`, `0x497020`): 448 at 9:00 rising 128 every six game hours (`0x107ac0` ms, a game hour being three real minutes), 0 at noon, so `a = (t - 0.5) * 512`, and the moon's angle half a turn on (`0x496f9a`, `0x497167`). So the sun's direction toward it is `(0, -sin 2 pi t, -cos 2 pi t)`, rising at -Y, highest at noon, setting at +Y, and the moon stands opposite it. Not settled: the moon's light in classic zones ([Point lights](#point-lights)).
- **Drawing** (fixed function, alpha-blended, never fogged, in this order): the dome, a sphere about the camera whose pole is the sun, colored by rings of angle from the pole (the pole and 0.01 radians row 0, ring r at `0.1 + r (pi - 0.2) / 29` row r + 1, the bottom row 29); the stars; the sun and moon, each a textured quad at distance 10 (`Distance`) with half size `Size`, square to its direction `-(sin H sin a, cos H sin a, cos a)`, `a = 2 pi t + Angle`; the clouds; the backdrop ring (`backdrops.ini`, only North Karana and Freeport East and West); and last the horizon band. The band is opaque fog color below elevation `a`, fades across the next `h` radians to row 30 at its own alpha (0 in the default maps), and draws nothing above; `a` and `h` move from their minimum to their maximum as the camera rises from `MinCameraZ` to `MaxCameraZ`.
- **In the preview:** the dome, sun, moon, and band, unfogged, as an equirectangular world image for the camera's height; beyond the far clip the sky shows, as in the client. Not drawn: clouds (placed at random by the client and always moving), stars, the backdrop ring, and the dome's and band's columns by azimuth (column 0 throughout; `DefaultDay`'s columns differ by at most 13 levels). The satellites' turn about their own axis and their fade are not traced.

## Calibration

`calibrateShot` renders a screenshot's view as passes (lit, texture, normal, baked light, share, distance) and solves the classic lighting and fog formula over a sample of the pixels: for each sun direction (and, without the zone header, each fog start and end at the client's density), the ambient, sun, bounce (and fog) colors are linear in every pixel and are solved by least squares per channel. Two ambiguities are settled by convention, not measured:

- Special ambient adds exactly like ambient where every surface takes the full share of scene light, so it is folded into ambient.
- A sun from one direction lights exactly as a bounce from the opposite one, so the sun is searched above the horizon and light from below is bounce.

Where no surface in view is fully fogged, a slightly nearer fog end with a slightly darker fog color draws nearly the same image, so a fitted fog is only as precise as the shot allows. The passes come back premultiplied by coverage and with negative values clamped to 0: the normal pass is encoded as normal * 0.5 + 0.5, and colors are divided by coverage before fitting.

A zone with a sky takes its light and fog color from it: Highpass Hold's midday RoF2 screenshot, drawn with its sky at 13:00 and its header's fog (200 to 1500 at 0.33), redraws within about 16.9 levels of 255 against 46.6 with hand-set light, 2.6 in the sky; what remains is mostly surface detail.

Results: the two Plane of Knowledge night shots redraw within about 11-13 levels of 255 on average (no sky drawn), and the Eastern Wastes day shot, with its fog fitted (start near 0, end near 1900), within about 16.

RoF2 shots of 2026-10-04 at hour 1 and 13, the owner's settings (advanced lighting off, so the single-pass effects; bloom and post effects change nothing visible at night), compared with `compareToConcept` at the MQPeridotEmu dumps' cameras (vertical field of view 37.666), each zone's sky at 1:00 and 13:00, and the character's carried light (light 14): averaged over 32-pixel blocks, the night shots of Freeport West differ by about 20 (the gate passage), 14 (the smithy's west face), 15 (the street south of it), and 8 (beside a torch) levels of 255, where without the carried light and the ambient floor they differed by 17, 17, 23, and 26; the walls take the shots' warm light, and what remains is mostly the preview drawing lighter, by the `MPL` materials ([EQG zones](#eqg-zones)), which also leave its noon shots 13 to 41 levels lighter. The Plane of Knowledge's night shot differs by about 10, nearly all the moon's blue on the ground ([Point lights](#point-lights), not settled), and its noon shot by about 6.
