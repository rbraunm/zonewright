# Preparing a zone for export

A game export writes what players' clients load (the `.eqg` and its side files) and what the server loads (its maps, nav mesh, and rows as SQL), and refuses what is not yet decided (README, Export). This is the procedure that decides it, in order. Every step ends in the picture it names: make it, open it, say in words what it shows, and decide from it before going on. The numbers (`checkExport`, `getRegions`, `mapReach`'s JSON) say where to look; they never stand in for the picture, and no other sweep of screenshots is taken.

**Whose decision.** The zone's server identity is the owner's: `shortName`, `zoneId`, `longName`, `timeType`, `entryGate`, and `serverTemplate` are asked of the owner, never guessed and never filled with a placeholder. A new zone takes its own id and short name, never an existing slot (the rows of an existing slot would clash): an id the client does not register, 787 to 999 but 997 (EQEmu's new-zone process), and `eqstrID`, a client string id `eqstr_us.txt` does not hold, for the client's entry. Everything else here is design, decided in the work file like any other pass: the safe point and underworld, entries, access, swim volumes, boundaries, and zone lines.

**Where exports go.** To a staging folder under the tooling root, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\zonewright\staging\<short>\<short>.eqg` (create the folder: `checkExport` and `exportZone` take a path in an existing folder whose name is the short name), never into the client folder (both refuse it) and never into the work repository. Deploying is part of the hand-off: the export's server side goes to claudeperidot, Claude's own test EQEmu server, only with `deployZone`, which writes, stages, and applies its changes through the change ledger (`claudeperidot/`; README, Deploying). Peridot is the owner's server: its hand-off goes to the owner, and Claude never applies anything to Peridot and never copies into the owner's client folder.

## Steps

1. **Recovery point, and the plan as it stands.** `saveFile`, then `saveCheckpoint` labelled "before export prep". Read what is decided: `getRegions` (its `undecided` list), `getEntries`, `getZoneLines`, `getBoundaries`, `getWater`, `getSwimVolumes`, and `getSceneSummary`'s zone properties. Picture: `renderSketch` over the whole zone with `sheets` `[]` (the sketch sheets, drawn by default, bury the plan's own layers under their labels): regions filled by access, entries, zone lines, boundaries, water, and swim volumes. The plan is 1440 x 810 with +X up and `width` running across it, so a zone long in X needs a `width` of about 1.8 times its X extent to fit. Say what is decided and what is missing.
2. **Owner values, safe point, underworld.** Ask the owner for every missing one of `shortName`, `zoneId` (a new zone's: 787 to 999 but 997, one the client does not register; the client registers none of 900 to 995), `longName`, `timeType`, `entryGate` (open, a minimum account status, or a zone flag), and `serverTemplate` (a zone the client registers, of the same kind, whose row a new zone row copies the rest from), and set them with `setZoneProperties`, which refuses an id or short name the client registers to another zone and an unregistered id under 787; choose `eqstrID` (a string id the client's `eqstr_us.txt` does not hold; setZoneProperties refuses one holding another string) and set it too. Then `checkServerZoneSlot` with `servers` `["claudeperidot", "peridot"]`: it refuses an id or short name the client or either server's zone table already gives another zone. Its result gives the sky's chain: the client picks the sky by the short name, so a stored sky type resolving to another sky than the short name's is refused by a game export. Set any missing view values (sky, fog, clips) by author-zone step 2. Then decide the safe point (where players arrive when nothing else places them: on the main arrival's floor, facing into the zone) and the underworld (below the lowest floor players can reach, such as the terrain's world minimum in `getObjectDetail` less a margin). Picture: the safe point's view, `renderView` `standAt` its point facing its heading (sketch footprints with heights stand in it as grey blocks; they are guides and never ship), with `getEntries` saying it stands `onFooting`.
3. **Ways in.** For every neighbour's zone line that leads here, `placeEntry` a `zoneIn` with `fromZone` and, when the neighbour row's `zone_points` number is known, `fromNumber` (the export then writes `<short>_waysIn.sql`; without it the check lists the way in for the server); for every port (a teleport door, an NPC port), a `landing`, `isolated` when only its port reaches it. Picture: each arrival view `placeEntry` returns (the scale figure walks ahead and drops out of the frame where the ground falls away, so look at the ground under the eye), then `renderSketch` with `sheets` `[]` framed round the entries: S the safe point, zoneIn with its zone, landing, T<number> for a teleport's landing, and each plot's entrance, every arrow facing the way players should look.
4. **Access and bodies.** Give every region in `getRegions`' `undecided` its access with `editRegion`: `play`, `view` (seen, never entered), or `none` (never reached). Confirm which body groups (small, medium, large; playerScale) the zone is built for (`setZoneProperties` `builtFor`, every group unless set) and give a region its own `builtFor` where its design is for other bodies (a gnome quarter, a giants' hall). All ground players can reach must lie in some region, so a backdrop gets a `view` or `none` region of its own (`createRegion`). Picture: `renderSketch` over the whole zone, `sheets` `[]`: no grey hatch left, view hatched amber, none hatched red, play outlined.
5. **Swimming, per body.** For each pool and river `getWater` lists: `buildSwimVolumes` or `placeSwimVolume`, adjusted by hand, then `acceptSwimVolumes`; or `editWater` with swimmable false for a fountain or a trickle (author-zone step 4). Picture: `renderSection` across each body with its swim volumes, and `renderSketch`'s swim layer. A zone with no pools or rivers has nothing to decide here.
6. **Exits and boundaries, by design.** Where the design leads out of the zone (a road, a canyon's end, a tunnel), `placeZoneLine` in the gap with its target (a zone the client registers, or this zone's `shortName` for a teleport or a fall catcher; each part of the point and the heading may be "keep"). Where nothing is meant to stop players but the ground does not, `placeBoundaryWall` at a rim's foot or along an open edge, and `placeBoundaryPlane` lids over gaps players could climb out of and floors under drops. Picture: `renderSection` on a line crossing each one (across a wall, through a zone line's box), with `bottom` and `top` spanning it, which draws the wall from under the ground to its top or the box against the ground; a line running along a wall cuts nothing of it, and a frame left to fit the ground clips a wall's or a box's top. Step 7 finds the rest.
7. **Reach.** `mapReach` over the whole zone. Picture 1, the lane plan; picture 2, the problem sheet. Say in words where each body group gets (a face up to its steepest, at least 80 degrees for every group, is walked, so canyon walls and scarps under that are climbed; the plan shows the large group's lane when the zone is built for every group, so its pink squeeze is where an ogre does not fit; `bodyGroup` draws another), where the leak edges run, and which regions they reach. Then `mapReach` by `level` where floors stack (a cave under a hill, an arch over a gorge), and framed with `center` and `width` on each area with problems. For each failure kind, look at its problem view and `traceReach` to it: reached, its route ribbon and decisive moves' frames show how players got there; unreached, its section and view name what separates. Fix it by design:
   - `voidEdge`, `belowUnderworld`, `noneRegion`: a wall, a lid, a floor, a zone line, or reshaping; never a region drawn round the leak to hide it.
   - `outsideRegions`: where the ground is meant to be reached or seen, the plan missed it, so extend a region's outline or draw one with its access; otherwise keep players off it as above.
   - `disconnectedEntry`, `unreachedPlot` with verdict `fails`: make the way for the groups that fail it (grade it, a span, stairs, more headroom or width), mark an entry only its port reaches `isolated`, or, where the place is meant for other bodies, say so with its region's `builtFor`; with verdict `finding`, only groups the place is not built for miss it.

   Then `mapReach` again: changed regions or access reuse the lane (it is judged against them after the flood), while a change to the ground, the boundaries, the swim volumes, the zone lines, the seeds, or the underworld computes it again. Each run numbers its problems afresh (a fixed problem's number passes to the next), so across runs name a problem by its kind and place; a framed plan rings only problems whose center lies in its frame, and a long void edge is ringed at its middle. Repeat until no failure kind is left. The findings (traps, reach into `view` regions, unreached zone lines, swim volumes, and play floor, undecided water, squeeze reach) are decided in step 10.
8. **Coverage.** `checkExport` with purpose `test` on the staging path lists the coverage findings without building the server side, each with the centers of its pieces. Picture: `renderView` with shading `coverage` at those places, from above (a `map` view over a piece's center) and at eye height: magenta a ground border without a transition strip, yellow texture stretched or squeezed, red zero texture area, blue a face seen from its back, orange the base material showing, brown blockout. Fix by design (`paintTransition`, `projectUVs`, `paintSurface`, `assignMaterial`, `cleanupMesh`), check, and look again.
9. **Looks.** `renderReviewSet` and the `review-zone` skill with its critic; then `viewFromLane`: a tour (where players actually stand: a tour standing in `view` regions shows reach the design did not mean) and each entry's surroundings, with `target` the entry's point from `getEntries` (an entry is an arrow empty with no surface to judge by name). Picture: the review sheet, the tour sheet, and each entry's sheet.
10. **Dry run.** `checkExport` with purpose `game` on the staging path, until it lists no failure. Picture: `mapReach`'s plan made after the last change (`checkExport` draws none; its failures and findings carry the numbers a `mapReach` run gives the same scene). Read every finding and decide each, fixing it or accepting it with the reason, which goes in the hand-off: NPC islands (players reach them; snap risk), failed NPC path probes (a zone line's own box is Disabled in the nav, so a probe to the center of a box more than 20 across finds no goal polygon within the server's 10), traps, reach into `view` regions, unreached zone lines, swim volumes, and play floor, undecided water, squeeze reach, a zone line in a swim volume, no zone lines, no `zoneIn` entry, an id the client does not register (the client needs a registration entry before it loads the zone), a reused slot, coverage, and the lane's limits. Its `serverFiles` says where the check built the files (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\zonewright\exports\<short>\check`) and the rows: `zone_points` numbers written and deleted, and `waysInToAdd`.
11. **Export.** `saveFile`, then `exportZone` with purpose `game` on the staging path. It writes the archive, its side files, `server\`, and the manifest `<short>_export.json`, every file with its size and SHA-256. Its pictures are step 12's, drawn from what it wrote.
12. **Verify.** `verifyExport` on the exported `<short>.eqg`. Picture: the server's view, drawn from the exported files alone (collision relief with upright walls and steep faces marked, the `.wtr` boxes, the NPC nav's main piece and islands, the safe point, `zone_points` positions, landings, and ways in), and the comparison sheets (scene, archive, and difference per review camera and tour eye, the views that draw otherwise first). Describe both. A failed check (`failedChecks`) goes back to the step that decides it, then steps 10 to 12 again; verifyExport opens the saved work file again when it is done.
13. **Deploy and hand-off note.** `deployZone` with the staging `.eqg`, `target` `claudeperidot`, the plan step the zone's work is on, `author` (this session's id, and the subagent's), `ownerClearance` (`pending` unless the owner's words cleared it), and `disposition` (`exploration`, or `fixture` when the owner keeps it): it installs the maps, makes the zone's rows exactly the export's through the ledger, and restarts the server; read its `notDeployed`. The client side follows EQEmu's documented route for now: the export's `clientEntry` (eq-core-dll's `ZoneEntry` line, and the `eqstr_us.txt` line with string 0's new count) and the `.eqg` with its side files go into the spare client's folder as ledger client changes; zonewright's own client tools replace that later. Then load the zone in the spare through claudelink and look: take claudelink shots there (the `calibrate-renderer` skill's procedure, at the review cameras' places and the looks views) and run `compareShot` on each against the open work file and against the deployed `.eqg`, reading its sheet and `zoneDrift` (the file's view values against the zone row the server sent). For Peridot, write the note to the owner:
    - the zone (`shortName`, `zoneId`, `longName`), the staging folder, and each file with its SHA-256 from the manifest;
    - `<short>.eqg` and its client side files (`<short>_EnvironmentEmitters.txt`, `<short>_assets.txt`, where the zone has them) go into each player's client folder; `<short>_export.json`, `<short>_housing.json` (the plots for the server's housing), and `server\` do not;
    - `server\maps` mirrors the server's own `maps` folder (`base`, `water`, `nav`) and is copied into it whole;
    - `server\sql\<short>.sql` is applied with the `mariadb` client; `<short>_waysIn.sql` only if the neighbours' rows are to change, since it rewrites another zone's `zone_points` rows; `<short>_pieces.sql`, where the zone has interactive pieces, with them (its doors and object rows name the copies in the archive); the ways in without a neighbour row (`waysInToAdd`) are to be added on the server; every guard in the files refuses and rolls back rather than overwrite a row that is not zonewright's;
    - world reads the zone table when it boots, and a zone process loads its maps and zone points when it boots, so world and the zone processes are restarted after applying;
    - the entry gate, and for a zone flag that each character needs the flag granted;
    - the template whose row the new zone row copies its other columns from;
    - the client's entry for a zone the client does not register (the manifest's `clientEntry`: the `ZoneEntry` line and the `eqstr_us.txt` lines);
    - the findings accepted in step 10, each with its reason, and that `verifyExport` passed all eleven checks, with the paths of its pictures.

    Claude never applies anything to Peridot and never copies into a client folder; the owner applies the hand-off.
